# MATC70 - Matrix Theory

MATC70 Mathematics: Matrix Theory, 7.5 credits, is an optional first-cycle course for a Bachelor of Science or Master of Science degree in mathematics. The course is taught in English.

The course is given jointly in several versions. This repository follows the 7.5-credit MATC70 version for students at the Faculty of Science. MATC70 has the same course content as FMAN71 and includes Chapter 10 of the course textbook.

## Course content

The course covers:

- Matrices and determinants
- Vector spaces and linear maps
- Spectral theory and the Jordan normal form
- Matrix factorizations
- Matrix polynomials and matrix functions
- Vector and matrix norms
- Inner products
- Singular values and normal matrices
- Quadratic and Hermitian forms
- Pseudoinverses and least-squares problems
- Positive and non-negative matrices

## Learning outcomes

After completing the course, the student should be able to:

- Characterize and use different matrix factorizations
- Explain matrix functions, particularly polynomials, and their connection to the Jordan normal form
- Describe, compute, and estimate vector and matrix norms, with and without computer support
- Describe common classes of normal matrices and their properties
- Combine methods from different parts of the course to solve matrix-theory problems
- Write programs that solve mathematical problems from the course
- Explain mathematical solutions clearly in speech and writing

## Assessment

The course is assessed through:

- Two mandatory programming assignments, called miniprojects in the detailed programme
- A written home examination worth 6 credits
- An oral examination worth 1.5 credits

Both miniprojects must be submitted on time and approved before the home examination is corrected. The oral examination may only be taken after passing the written home examination.

The grading scale is Fail, Pass, and Pass with distinction. Passing the course requires approved miniprojects and passing both the home and oral examinations. The final grade combines the results of the home and oral examinations.

The dates and detailed rules for the 2026 home and oral examinations have not yet been provided.

### December 2024 home examination

The [December 2024 home examination](exams/oldexamdec2024.pdf) is included as a past paper and is also the source of exercises T.1-T.8 in the 2026 course programme. Its rules should not be assumed to apply unchanged in 2026.

In that examination, students on a 7.5-credit course could submit solutions to at most seven of eight problems. Books and computer programs were permitted, but assistance from other people or chatbots was prohibited.

## Assignments

The voluntary assignments are optional, award no bonus, and are intended to provide feedback on written solutions and applications of the theory. Partial submissions are permitted.

The miniprojects are compulsory. The syllabus describes them as programming assignments, while the detailed programme calls them miniprojects.

For Miniproject 1, this repository follows the Python version. See the [project description](mini_project_1/description.pdf) and [supplied test matrices](mini_project_1/testmatrices.py).

## Entry requirements

Admission requires at least 67.5 credits in mathematics and numerical analysis, including:

- MATB22 Linear Algebra 2, 7.5 credits
- NUMA01 Computational Programming with Python, 7.5 credits
- Either MATB13 Discrete Mathematics, 7.5 credits, or MATB24 Linear Analysis, 7.5 credits
- Equivalent prior study may also satisfy the requirements
